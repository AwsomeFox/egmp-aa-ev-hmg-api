# Fill the energy model before chasing protocols

**Status, 2026-11-21.** This is the research from 2026-11-21, plus the off-car work that followed it the same day. What that work changed:
- **Sensor 23, done in this branch:** Google's VEM v1.2 names are in `reference/vehicle_energy_model.proto`. `1.2` is mass (2200 kg). `1.5` hvac_status and `1.8` battery temperature are live. `1.10` is set. See `docs/ENERGY-MODEL.md`, "Google's schema (VEM v1.2)".
- **Sensor 26, settled.** The curve's meaning was already proven in Maps' code and on the car (2026-11-10). A Maps 26.44 decompile names its two int fields: `1` = minimum arrival % at charging stops, `2` = target arrival % at the destination. They are now the `arrival_charger_pct` / `arrival_dest_pct` settings. The "Sensor 26" section below is kept as written, with this correction.
- **Connector numbering, still a car test.** Maps' internal enum is J1772 = 2, CCS1 = 5, NACS = 14, against the AA wire's J1772 = 1, CCS1 = 4. Something has to translate between them, presumably the Android Auto app, which wasn't decompiled. Emulating the AA library shows its connector validator accepts only 0–9 and 101, so NACS (14) needs a one-byte recipe change (`cmp r0,#9` to `#14` at vaddr 0x1401c2 of library 48ee793a).
- **0x8008 (energy forecast).** Emulated on the turn-by-turn library, the nav endpoint returns −253 for it, with no callbacks, and keeps working.
- **Turn-by-turn.** The 2026-11-03 drive captures show one step per 0x8006, the same text in step fields 2 and 4, and no current road in 0x8007. No protocol version above 1.6 adds turn data. A full Java decoder (`AaNavState`) is in the Wireless mod's `feat/aa-nav-state` branch.

The "Sibling reports" this cites (intersections, ch0iiih's fixes) are separate research notes, not in this repo.

The biggest gains for the Ioniq 5 (NEEV23) do not need a newer Android Auto protocol. Our receiver already asks for protocol 1.6, and at 1.6 the phone already sends the full modern turn-by-turn set (0x8006 steps, lanes, cue text and destinations, including **per-stop charging details**; 0x8007 distances, time-to-step and ETAs). What limits the cluster and HUD today is how much of that we decode, not what the phone sends. On EV routing, egmp-aa-ev already does what Ford's 2024 integration does: it advertises an electric car, streams a vehicle energy model (sensor 23) and preconditions from Maps' own charging plan. The best next step is to **fill the energy-model fields that egmp marks "unknown"**. A leaked Google schema (VEM v1.2) lines up with egmp's wire layout wherever egmp has proven a field, and it names most of the unknowns: 1.2 is vehicle mass (we send 2400 as a "reserve"), 1.5 is HVAC state, 1.8 is battery temperature, block 4 is *max regen power* (we treat 4.1 as max charge power), 6 is aux drain over time, 9 is charging time while plugged in, and 10 is the battery-preconditioning model. These changes are payload-only. They carry no risk to the head unit and can be A/B-tested on routes. The one newer protocol message with real EV value, VehicleEnergyForecast (0x8008), needs a requested version of at least 5.1. That brings ackless audio (5.0) and a new media message (0x8014) into the stock audio/video pipeline, so it belongs in a test build only. Cluster video is a dead end on Gen5W. On CarPlay, route guidance is done. EV data over iAP2 gets Apple Maps' "find a charger" prompts but not full EV routing, which needs Apple–OEM enrollment.

**How claims are tagged.** **[DOC]** means Google, Apple, AOSP or OEM documentation (including Google's leaked-but-labelled VEM schema, marked [DOC-leak]). **[RE]** means reverse-engineered by a named open-source project, or by our own docs from decompiled Maps. **[CODE]** means what our own repos do today, read from source at the commits named in the inventory (egmp-aa-ev `release/0.8.2` @ `f90c693`; Wireless CarPlay + AA mod `origin/main` @ `f657ca4`, 2.2.0-rc.9; HMG-API `origin/main` and PR #221). **[SPEC]** means inference or speculation that needs a test. Repo paths below are relative to each repo's root: `egmp` = egmp-aa-ev-hmg-api, `Wireless` = hmgapi-wireless-carplay-aa, `HMG-API` = HMG-API.

Two sibling reports cover adjacent ground and are not repeated here: **intersections** (junction geometry and side streets for the cluster and AR HUD) and **ch0iiih-fixes** (bugs in the Wireless mod's TBT path, found by comparing it with ch0iiih's fixes). This report agrees with both where they overlap, and says so at those points.

## Our 1.6 receiver already gets the modern nav set; everything newer sits behind 4.3–5.1 gates

### Version negotiation is raw, and the phone gates features on what we asked for

The version exchange is plain binary before TLS. The head unit sends `VERSION_REQUEST` (0x0001) as `u16 major, u16 minor`, and the phone answers with its version plus a match status. In AA 17.3, the phone answers **1.7 to any request up to 1.7 and 6.1 to anything higher**, but it keeps the head unit's *raw requested* version in `CarInfo` and checks every feature gate against that value [RE] ([OOA version/SSL](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/docs/interactions/02-version-ssl-auth.md); [OOA gates](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/analysis/reports/gal-version-gates-above-4.3.md)). The stock HMG receiver library asks for 1.4. The Wireless mod's in-memory recipe for `libautoreceiver_infobank.so` patches the minor immediate in `Controller::sendVersionRequest` (`00 01 00 01 00 04` → `…06`), so the car asks for **1.6** [CODE] (`Wireless: wireless-carplay-aa/recipes/aa-tbt-48ee793a.recipe:1-16`, `tools/ceev-tbt/README.md`). Only the minor is patched today. Any jump past 1.x would also need the major bytes in the same immediate changed [SPEC].

OOA's static decompile of AA 17.3 finds these gates [RE] ([OOA gates](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/analysis/reports/gal-version-gates-above-4.3.md)):

| Requested | What changes on the phone | Matters to us? |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4 | Unknown gate | — |
| **1.6** | **0x8006/0x8007 modern nav messages; REROUTING (status 3) allowed**; WireConfig added to the version response | **Already have it** |
| 4.3 | `AdditionalVideoConfig.hidden_ui_elements` replaces the SDR `session_configuration` clock/signal/battery bits; UIElement 5 becomes the "cluster turn card" flag | Risky: the stock UI-hiding bits would stop working |
| 4.5 | `KEYCODE_TEL` opens the dialer | No |
| **5.0** | **Audio becomes ackless** (no per-frame ack flow control); the audio start indication gains session type and media config | **High risk to the stock audio pipeline** |
| **5.1** | **VehicleEnergyForecast (nav 0x8008)** and standalone audio `MediaOptions` (media 0x8014) | The only EV gain |
| 6.0 | Video media options, H.265 path, frame-timing changes | Risk to video; our own tests found AA fps limited by the 4-unacked-frame video window |
| 6.1 | Response ceiling | — |

None of the gates above 1.7 has been confirmed with a live capture. OOA marks all of them static-only, and recommends a head unit step up one gate at a time (4.3, then 5.0, then 5.1) rather than jumping to 6.1 [RE].

### "Navigation status 3" is an enum value, not a service version

The Wireless recipe's header reads "protocol 1.6, navigation status 3". That phrase has caused confusion, including in our own protocol notes, which guessed it was a hidden library version constant. It is neither a version nor a field. The recipe's patch at `0x144efa 03` widens the library's `NavigationStatus_NavigationStatusEnum_IsValid` so it accepts **value 3, REROUTING**. The stock validator stopped at 2, so the library rejected 0x8003 messages carrying a reroute [CODE] (`Wireless: tools/ceev-tbt/README.md`). The navigation-status service descriptor has only three fields in every open-source proto: `minimum_interval_ms`, `type` (1/2) and optional `image_options` {height, width, colour_depth_bits}. **There is no version field to advertise** [RE] ([AAP protos.proto](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto); [OOA NavigationChannelData.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/navigation/NavigationChannelData.proto)). What unlocks 0x8006/0x8007 is the protocol minor bump from 4 to 6. The phone also suppresses REROUTING for a head unit below 1.6, so the two patches belong together [RE] ([OOA NavigationStateMessage.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/navigation/NavigationStateMessage.proto)). The intersections report uses the same "navigation status 3" wording, and it reaches the same conclusion that no later version adds step fields.

The two RE projects agree on wire numbers but **disagree on names**. In 0x8006, step field 2 is "road" in aasdk/aa-proxy and "instruction" in OOA, and field 4 is "cue alternate_text" in one and "road_names" in the other. They also disagree on the 0x8004 field order and on maneuver values 30/31 [RE] ([OOA NavigationNotificationMessage.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/navigation/NavigationNotificationMessage.proto)). The modern maneuver enum runs 0–50. Its values match the Car App Library's `Maneuver.TYPE_*` constants 1:1 (KEEP_LEFT 3, the slight/normal/sharp turns and ramps, FORK/MERGE sides, roundabout CW/CCW with exit number and angle, DESTINATION_LEFT/RIGHT) [DOC] ([Maneuver reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/car/app/navigation/model/Maneuver)) [RE] ([OOA nav.md](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/docs/channels/nav.md)). Google says `updateTrip` data from any Car App Library nav app "can be used by the vehicle's cluster and heads-up displays", so what the Wireless mod does with this channel applies to Waze and third-party apps as well as Google Maps [DOC] ([Build a navigation app](https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps/navigation)).

One conflict is settled by our own car test. OOA models 0x8006 `destinations[].2` as `ChargingStationDetails {connector_count, available_count, max_power_kw}`. egmp decoded it on the car on 2026-10-29 as **{departure energy Wh, charger max power W, planned charging seconds}**, which is the same shape as the forecast's `EnergyChargingStationDetails` [CODE] (`egmp: docs/PRECONDITIONING.md:203-211`, `gpm-ev/src/.../NavProto.java:8-28`). The car capture wins. This matters for the EV section: **at 1.6 we already get Maps' charging plan for every stop.**

### Sensors, car identity and the newer services

`SensorType` now runs from 1 to 26. Types 23–26 are VEHICLE_ENERGY_MODEL (23), TRAILER (24), RAW_VEHICLE_ENERGY_MODEL (25) and RAW_EV_TRIP_SETTINGS (26). `SensorBatch` uses the type number as its field number [RE] ([OOA SensorTypeEnum.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/sensor/SensorTypeEnum.proto); [AARE gearhead-notes](https://github.com/prototux/AARE/blob/main/docs/research/gearhead-notes.md)). The sensor service descriptor carries `supported_fuel_types` (field 3) and `supported_ev_connector_types` (field 4) [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto)). For identity, make, model, year and the head unit's strings moved from deprecated top-level discovery fields into `HeadUnitInfo` (field 17). AA 16.2 added `vehicle_type` (0 = car, 2 = truck), which is of no use to us [RE] ([OOA HeadUnitInfoData.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/control/HeadUnitInfoData.proto)). Discovery can also carry `ConnectionConfiguration`, which sets ping timing and wireless TCP buffer sizes [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto)). That knob is untested on wireless AA here.

AA 17.3 adds four optional services [RE] ([OOA services.md](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/analysis/reports/android-auto-17.3-update/services.md)). They are CarControl (15: property configs and controls), CarLocalMedia (16), BufferedMedia (17) and CarIntent (18). CarIntent parses a head-unit-to-phone "NAVIGATE" string behind a flag that defaults to off. CarControl is the one most likely to matter later. It is a plausible carrier for a phone-to-car command such as Ford's en-route preconditioning, but no source ties the two together [SPEC]. `ServiceDiscoveryUpdate` can change only the input endpoint after setup, so nothing can be added to video or nav mid-session [RE] ([OOA ServiceDiscoveryUpdateMessage.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/control/ServiceDiscoveryUpdateMessage.proto)).

On input, media and Wi-Fi, the newer items are small for our purposes. Input keycodes now go above 65536 (ROTARY 65536, NAVIGATION 65538, TEL 65540, TURN_CARD 65544). The codec list adds VP9, AV1 and H.265, and the H.265 encoder path is behind the 6.0 gate. Media message IDs 0x800C–0x8015 add overlay, head-unit UI config, media stats, MediaOptions and critical-UI notices [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto)). For Wi-Fi projection, OOA's APK-derived RFCOMM messages number fields from 2 and add `supportedWifiChannels`/`selectedWifiChannelType`. The aasdk lineage numbers from 1, and a capture would settle which one the HMG stack uses [RE] ([OOA wifi-projection.md](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/docs/channels/wifi-projection.md)). The Wireless repo's `feat/wifi-direct` branch is the place for that work [CODE].

## egmp already does Ford-style live routing; the gaps are fields Maps reads server-side

### What Google shipped, and what a head unit must advertise

Google has two EV paths on Android Auto. The **live-data path** launched at CES on 9 January 2024 with the Mustang Mach-E and F-150 Lightning. It shows battery on arrival, suggests charging stops and estimates charging time for the specific vehicle [DOC] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/09/google-maps-will-now-give-ev-drivers-real-time-battery-range-info-starting-with-ford/); [Google CES24](https://blog.google/products/android/android-auto-new-features-ces24/)). Ford's page adds multi-stop planning, charge-time ETAs, "Dynamic Charger Filtering by speed, charging network, and more", and **En-Route Preconditioning**: Maps "will trigger automatic preconditioning of your vehicle's battery as it approaches a known DC Fast Charger." Battery data updates only while Android Auto is connected [DOC] ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/support/how-tos/electric-vehicles/other-electric-vehicle-information/how-do-i-use-android-auto-ev-routing-for-ford-vehicles)). The **manual-entry path** rolled out from 30 March 2026 for **over 350 US models**, Hyundai and Kia included. The user types the current charge level, Maps 25.44 or newer is required, and Android Authority states plainly that "this is not a live feed from your car" [DOC] ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/google-maps-simplifies-battery-predictions-and-trip-planning-for-350-android-auto-ev-models/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-maps-android-auto-ev-trip-planning-3653027/)). Because it has no live battery data, Maps cannot update that plan as you drive [DOC] ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/android-phones/google-maps-just-added-a-major-ev-charging-update-and-it-fixes-one-of-my-biggest-electric-driving-problems)).

The manual path needs nothing from the head unit. The live path needs the head unit to **advertise sensor 23, fuel type ELECTRIC and EV connectors**, after which the phone requests sensor 23 and forwards its bytes opaquely to Maps [RE] ([OAL EV RE](https://github.com/mossyhub/openautolink/blob/main/docs/ev-energy-model-reverse-engineering.md); [aa-proxy-rs ev.rs](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/ev.rs)). The `ENHANCED_NAVIGATION_METADATA` capability is not required for the energy model [RE] ([OAL](https://github.com/mossyhub/openautolink/blob/main/docs/ev-energy-model-reverse-engineering.md)). No partner allowlist is visible: openautolink got live SOC into Maps on a Chevrolet Blazer EV through a non-GM head unit, and aa-proxy-rs works on arbitrary cars [RE] ([aa-proxy docs](https://aa-proxy.github.io/docs/ev)). egmp advertises exactly these (`egmp: hmgapi-mod/mod.json:39-42`; sensor 23 is added in `HmgApiEntry.java:203-229`) [CODE]. Nobody has tested what Maps does when a user has also set up the March 2026 manual car profile on the same phone [SPEC]. It belongs on the car-test list.

For preconditioning, Ford documents the behaviour but not the message. No public source says how Maps triggers the car [DOC/RE gap]. The candidates are the car reading Maps' forecast (0x8008) or a CarControl/vendor message [SPEC]. egmp's approach does not depend on knowing. It reads **every destination's charging-stop details from 0x8006** and the next stop's time from 0x8007, picks the first charger above 50 kW that is within 60 minutes, and drives HMG-API's `conditioning` command through a relay service [CODE] (`egmp: gpm-ev/.../PrecondCore.java:75-131`, `hmgapi-mod/.../Precond.java:365-443`, `service/.../RelayService.java`). Our EV notes speculated that 0x8008 would "also cover intermediate stops, not just the final destination". That point is moot, because 0x8006 already lists Maps' intermediate charging stops.

### Sensor 23 field by field: the leaked schema names most of our unknowns

The AA app forwards sensor 23 bytes to Maps untouched. On the phone, Maps reads only current energy and capacity; **the energy prediction runs server-side** [RE] ([egmp-aa-ev-feat ENERGY-MODEL.md](https://github.com/willemcvu/egmp-aa-ev-feat/blob/main/docs/ENERGY-MODEL.md); [OAL contract audit](https://github.com/mossyhub/openautolink/blob/main/docs/research/ev-external-model-contract.md)). A Google `energy_model.proto` labelled "Vehicle energy model (v1.2)", Copyright 2020, is public in a third-party repo [DOC-leak] ([kangrio energy_model.proto](https://github.com/kangrio/CarService-AOSP/blob/main/proto/energy_model.proto)). Its numbering matches every position egmp has proven on the wire: 1.3/1.4 energy and capacity, 1.6 air density, 1.7 outside temperature, 2.x road load, 3.**7** drivetrain loss, 5.**4** battery loss, 7 aux by temperature, and 8.1/8.2 charging curves by power and by battery temperature. The odd sub-field numbers 3.7 and 5.4 matching is the strongest sign that the schema applies to what Maps parses [SPEC, strong]. openautolink's audit of Maps 26.21 independently confirms 1.8 = battery temperature, 1.9 = peak motor power, 1.10 = max charge rate, 1.11 = heat pump, and 1.2 = mass [RE] ([OAL audit](https://github.com/mossyhub/openautolink/blob/main/docs/research/ev-external-model-contract.md)).

The re-route thresholds egmp found in Maps fit the leaked names: `6[0].1` ≥ 600 and `6[0].3` ≥ 500 read naturally as aux-drain watts, and `10.2` ≥ 500 as preconditioning power in watts [SPEC]. The schema dates from 2020 and the partner reference page is not public [DOC gap], so every new field needs an A/B route test before it ships. Filling these fields cannot hurt the head unit. A wrong value can make Maps' plan worse, and only route comparisons will show it.

Sensors 6 and 5 are simpler. egmp sends fuel level as the **displayed SOC** (DISPLAY_SOC half-steps, rounded up), range as the DTE in metres and a low flag at 10% or below, all through GPM's own `nativeReportFuelData`. It sends the odometer through `reportOdometerData`, but not `trip_kms_e1` [CODE] (`egmp: hmgapi-mod/.../LiveData.java:86-127`, `HmgApiEntry.java:378-393`). The protos do not state units for `range` [RE gap]. GPM's JNI takes metres, so that is settled for us.

### Sensor 26: a well-formed container (meaning since settled; see Status)

egmp sends sensor 26 as `SensorBatch{26: {1: cdpz{1: cdqu{4[]: {3 float raw%, 4 float shown%}}}}}`, 13 points [CODE] (`egmp: hmgapi-mod/.../SocCurve.java:23-67`). Every protocol source names type 26 `RAW_EV_TRIP_SETTINGS {optional bytes raw_data = 1}`, "opaque blob, internal structure TBD" [RE] ([OOA RawEvTripSettingsData.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/sensor/RawEvTripSettingsData.proto)). openautolink calls it "EV trip configuration" [RE] ([OAL](https://github.com/mossyhub/openautolink/blob/main/docs/ev-energy-model-reverse-engineering.md)). The two views do not conflict on the wire. AA wraps an opaque message in field 1, and egmp's message sits in that field 1. egmp's docs say Maps (where Maps' sensor 25 is wire 26) parses the wrapped bytes as `cdpz`, and **honoured the curve only on wire 26, wrapped in field 1** [RE] (`egmp: docs/ENERGY-MODEL.md:214,221-231`). The open question is the meaning. If the Maps-side message is really a "trip settings" object (arrival-SOC target, charging preferences) that happens to contain a %-mapping table, our curve could be setting something besides the raw-to-shown mapping [SPEC]. One more clue: our docs record a Maps message `cdhl` with schema `{1 int32, 2 int32, 4[] {3 float, 4 float}}`, the same point shape as our curve. They file it under "sensor 25", which may be Maps' numbering for wire 26 [SPEC]. Sensor 26 also works only on the 48ee793a library recipe (the US Ioniq 5's), not on dbfe7bd2 or the `.sites` recipe [CODE].

**Recommended test.** Pick one fixed route with a charging stop and fixed starting SOC. Drive or plan it twice with only the `battery_curve` setting changed. Log the battery % Maps shows at start against the cluster's SOC, the arrival % at each stop and at the destination, and the 0x8006 `destinations[].2` departure Wh and planned charging seconds from NAVDUMP. If Maps' start % matches the cluster only with the curve on, and nothing else in the plan moves beyond that offset, the curve is doing what we think. If stop choice or departure energy changes more than the % offset explains, 26 is carrying trip settings and should be off by default until it is understood.

### Connectors: [4, 1] is right for CCS1 on the AA wire

Three numberings are in play. **AOSP VHAL** puts CCS1 at **5** (`IEC_TYPE_1_CCS_DC`) and J1772 at 1 [DOC] ([AOSP EvConnectorType.aidl](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/hardware/interfaces/+/refs/heads/main/automotive/vehicle/aidl_property/android/hardware/automotive/vehicle/EvConnectorType.aidl)). The **AA wire enum** in the aasdk lineage is UNKNOWN 0, J1772 1, MENNEKES 2, CHADEMO 3, **COMBO_1 4**, COMBO_2 5, TESLA_ROADSTER 6, TESLA_HPWC 7, TESLA_SUPERCHARGER 8, GBT 9, OTHER 101. OOA's APK decompile adds GBT_DC 10, LECCS 11 and **NACS 14** [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto); [OOA EVConnectorTypeEnum.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/sensor/EVConnectorTypeEnum.proto); [LIVI sensor.proto](https://github.com/f-io/LIVI/blob/main/native/crates/livi-aa-proto/proto/sensor.proto)). The **Car App Library** `EnergyProfile` uses the same numbers as the wire through 10 (COMBO_1 = 4) and has no NACS constant [DOC] ([EnergyProfile](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/car/app/hardware/info/EnergyProfile)). Our stock HMG library itself accepts connectors **0–9 and 101**, exactly the aasdk enum [CODE] (`egmp: docs/RESEARCH.md:74,81`). The strongest evidence therefore puts **CCS1 = 4 on the wire**, and egmp's `[4, 1]` is correct. openautolink passes VHAL `[1, 5]` straight through, which on the AA wire would advertise COMBO_2 (CCS2) [SPEC]. Our EV notes called that weak evidence for VHAL numbering; it is better read as a likely bug in that project. **Car test:** after connecting, check Maps' "Compatible Plugs" screen shows CCS1 and J1772, not CCS2. Two smaller corrections follow. egmp's docs equate NACS with type 8 (TESLA_SUPERCHARGER), but OOA's newer enum has NACS at 14, which the stock library would reject without widening its validator [CODE/RE]. For a 2023 Ioniq 5, NACS should stay a user opt-in for owners who carry an adapter, matching Ford's "Compatible Plugs" guidance [DOC] ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/support/how-tos/electric-vehicles/other-electric-vehicle-information/how-do-i-use-android-auto-ev-routing-for-ford-vehicles)).

### VehicleEnergyForecast (0x8008): real data behind a risky gate

0x8008 wraps a proto3 `VehicleEnergyForecast`: `energy_at_next_stop` and `distance_to_empty` (each distance m, arrival Wh, time s), `forecast_quality` (LOW/HIGH), `next_charging_stop` (min departure Wh, max power W, charging s), per-stop details and data authorizations [RE] ([OOA VehicleEnergyForecastMessage.proto](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/oaa/navigation/VehicleEnergyForecastMessage.proto)). The phone sends it only when the requested version is **at least 5.1 and a navigation service exists**. aa-proxy-rs injects a nav service for head units that lack one, because "without it the phone never sends VehicleEnergyForecast" [RE] ([aa-proxy-rs mitm.rs](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/mitm.rs)). An AA log string says the forecast also needs "enhanced nav metadata", but OOA lists that capability as deprecated and hardcoded on the phone [RE] ([OOA phenotype-flags.md](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/docs/phenotype-flags.md)). Compared with what 0x8006 already gives us, the new data is **arrival energy at the next stop, Maps' distance-to-empty and a forecast-quality flag**. Those would let preconditioning key on arrival SOC and would give the cluster Maps' own range figure.

The cost is the version. To reach 5.1, the head unit must pass 5.0, where the phone stops waiting for per-frame audio acks and extends the audio start indication. The stock receiver library was written for 1.x audio and might choke on the new start fields, or misbehave without the ack flow control it expects [SPEC]. At 5.1 the phone also sends media `MediaOptions` (0x8014), which the stock media endpoint has never seen [RE]. aa-proxy-rs handles this by raising the head unit's request in a proxy, then **consuming and dropping 0x8008 and 0x8014** before they reach a legacy head unit [RE] ([aa-proxy-rs mitm.rs](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/mitm.rs); [config.rs](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/config.rs)). We have no proxy, but we have the equivalent hooks. 0x8008 would land in the nav endpoint's unknown-message fallback, which is exactly where the Wireless recipe's injected parser sits, so the parser could dump it as hex the way it dumps 0x8006. 0x8014 would need a new drop at the media endpoint's fallback [SPEC]. Treat this as a staged test-build experiment (4.3, then 5.0, then 5.1), each step checked for audio dropouts, video fps and nav messages, before it goes anywhere near a release.

## Metadata, not video, is the Gen5W cluster path, and we decode only part of it

### What the phone sends versus what reaches the cluster and HUD

The Wireless mod's injected parser repacks 0x8006/0x8007 into GPM's two legacy JNI callbacks as extra event codes and flags (32 destination, 34 lanes, 64–67 turns ahead, distance flags 0x100/0x200/0x800). It also emits the whole message as a hex dump (event 33) [CODE] (`Wireless: AaTurns.java:22-41`). `AaTurns` turns that into HMG-API `Cluster` calls: `tbtInfo`, `crossroadName`, `destinationName`, `tbtSubInfo` (remaining distance and ETA), `turnList` (up to 3), the HUD's `turnListNew` 0xF2 and `tbtSubInfo2C`, and lanes as `laneInfo` 0xF1 (up to 8) [CODE] (`Wireless: AaTurns.java:330-827`).

| Available at 1.6 | Reaches cluster/HUD today [CODE] |
|---|---|
| Maneuver type 0–50 + roundabout exit number and angle | Collapsed to the legacy 0–19 event set plus side and angle. ROUNDABOUT_ENTER and FERRY_TRAIN are dropped as the current turn. UNKNOWN shows straight |
| Lanes (shape + highlighted) | Yes, 0xF1. Which arrow is highlighted is a likely bug; see the ch0iiih-fixes report #9 |
| Road name per step | Yes. The last multi-byte character is damaged; see the ch0iiih-fixes report #5 |
| Cue `alternate_text` | Not used |
| Current road (0x8007 field 3) | Not used |
| Time-to-step seconds | Ignored. Only trip seconds are used (`AaTurns.java:223-248`) |
| Destinations with ETA string and time-to-arrival | First destination name and remaining trip |
| Per-stop charging details (0x8006 dest.2) | Only in the NAVDUMP that egmp reads. Not shown on the cluster |

The Java maneuver table uses only the legacy enum (`AaTurns.java:32-37`) [CODE]. That means KEEP_LEFT/RIGHT, slight versus sharp ramps, fork and merge sides, DESTINATION_LEFT/RIGHT and roundabout direction all reach the cluster coarser than the phone sent them [SPEC, from the code]. The parser is a binary blob reused byte for byte from NEMESIS, with no source in the repo, so changing it is expensive [CODE] (`Wireless: tools/ceev-tbt/README.md`). The cheaper route is already half-built. The NAVDUMP hex carries the whole message. HMG-API's `navigationDump` parses it into an `aa_navigation_message` intent, and PR #221 adds `addNavigationListener` for hook code [CODE] ([HMG-API PR #221](https://github.com/Open-HMG/HMG-API/pull/221); `HMG-API: core/runtime/java/hmgapi/hook/ProjectionHooks.java:282-299`). egmp's `NavProto` already decodes part of it, and the intersections report notes that ch0iiih's `AaNavigationState` decodes the same dump. A full Java decoder would give `AaTurns` the exact maneuver, cue text, current road and time-to-step. If the dump holds untrimmed bytes, it would also sidestep the parser's string bug that the ch0iiih-fixes report proves [SPEC]. The intersections report reaches the same verdict for its own scope: the receiver already hands Java everything, and what `AaTurns` drops (cue text, later destinations) is not geometry. This report agrees, and adds that the dropped maneuver detail and time-to-step are worth recovering for the HUD.

### Cluster video exists, but Google names Hyundai, Kia and Genesis in its reject list

AA supports a second projected video sink with `display_type = DISPLAY_TYPE_CLUSTER`. AA 17.3 allows at most one, builds its own encoder and focus state for it, and **picks the content on the phone** (Maps' cluster service, another nav app's, or the Gearhead turn card, by power policy), not the head unit [RE] ([OOA multi-display 17.3](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/analysis/reports/multi-display/android-auto-17.3.md); [OOA cluster policy](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/analysis/reports/multi-display/android-auto-17.3-runtime-cluster-policy.md)). Google's docs say cluster displays reliably show only map tiles [DOC] ([Build a navigation app](https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps/navigation)). A phenotype flag, **`reject_clusters_for_unsupported_nav_apps`, defaults to "hyundai, kia, genesis"** [RE] ([OOA phenotype-flags.md](https://github.com/mrmees/open-android-auto/blob/main/docs/phenotype-flags.md)). Read literally, on HMG vehicles the phone refuses the cluster display when the active nav app has no cluster support, instead of falling back to the generic turn card. The flag is server-tunable, and the field it matches (discovery make or head-unit make) is unknown [SPEC]. For us it settles nothing, because the path is closed earlier. The Gen5W cluster takes `updateTBTInfo`-style frames, not video from the projection stack (the intersections report reaches the same conclusion). A second H.264 stream would also compete with the main display's limited unacked-frame window. That flag's existence hints that Google has seen HMG cars with AA cluster displays, perhaps ccNC, but no public Hyundai/Kia source confirms any [SPEC] ([Kia update notice](https://update.kia.com/US/EN/updateNoticeView/wqTX9K)).

### Nav focus, speed limits and cameras

The NavFocus handshake (control 13/14, NATIVE 1 / PROJECTED 2) decides who owns guidance [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto)). When the head unit holds NATIVE, Car App Library apps get `onStopNavigation` and must stop cluster output and voice [DOC] ([Build a navigation app](https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps/navigation)). The Wireless mod already stops `AaTurns` on NATIVE and replaces the car navigation's 13 TBT calls while a phone guides, leaving its speed limits and cameras in place [CODE] (`Wireless: Wireless.java:87-147,321-331`). No AA message carries speed limits, cameras or traffic signs in either direction [RE] ([AAP](https://github.com/aa-proxy/aa-proxy-rs/blob/main/src/protos/protos.proto)). Maps' speedometer with the local limit (a limited rollout from July 2026) is drawn inside the projected video [DOC/press] ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-maps-android-auto-live-speedometer-3689236/)), and Waze sends no hazards to clusters [DOC/press] ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/02/27/waze-update-instrument-cluster-display/)). Speed limits on the cluster and HUD must keep coming from the car's own map, as they do now. The empty distance bar under AA needs four new HMG-API trap-frame hook points, and sending trap data from GPM replaces the car's speed-limit sign [CODE] (`Wireless: docs/ar-hud.md:105-146`).

### What phone apps such as ABRP can read from our car

On Android Auto, the Car App Library's `CarInfo` is implemented by `ProjectedCarInfo`, which asks the AA host. That gives Model, EnergyProfile, EnergyLevel (battery %, range, low flag), Speed, Mileage and Toll exactly as far as the head unit supplies them. `ExteriorDimensions` always returns unknown, and `EvStatus` is still experimental [DOC] ([CarInfo](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/car/app/hardware/info/CarInfo); [ProjectedCarInfo.java](https://github.com/androidx/androidx/blob/androidx-main/car/app/app-projected/src/main/java/androidx/car/app/hardware/info/ProjectedCarInfo.java); [Car hardware APIs](https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps/library/car-hardware-api)). The likely mapping is EnergyLevel from sensor 6, EnergyProfile from the advertised fuel and connector lists, and Mileage from sensor 5 [SPEC]. ABRP staff confirm they "only pass along the values" the car provides [Forum] ([ABRP Featurebase](https://abrp.featurebase.app/p/dacia-spring-2024-in-android-auto-reports-soc-5-11percent)). Since egmp sends the displayed SOC, ABRP on our car should match the dashboard rather than show the Dacia-style offset, and should see CCS1 + J1772, because EnergyProfile shares the wire numbering [SPEC]. No app can see sensor 23; that is a Maps-only path. This has not been checked on the car.

### Other sessions

Intersection and side-street drawing is covered in **the intersections report**. It recommends Apple Maps junction angles on CarPlay first and the car's ADASIS v2 STUB horizon as a candidate source for AA. The HMG-API-tbt worktree (`tbt-lanes-ar`) carries related work. Bug-level TBT fixes (road text, stale route state, lane arrows, the HUD next icon) are in **the ch0iiih-fixes report** and the Wireless `fix/turns-quick-fixes` branch. ## CarPlay: route guidance is solved, EV routing needs Apple enrollment

### iAP2 route guidance already feeds our cluster

Four RE projects agree on the route-guidance message IDs: 0x5200 start, 0x5201 RouteGuidanceUpdate, 0x5202 RouteGuidanceManeuverUpdate, 0x5203 stop and 0x5204 LaneGuidanceInformation [RE] ([JJTech0130 dissector](https://gist.github.com/JJTech0130/78527c600f7b4d0a7aeb294eab06d8ba); [mib2q-carplay-rgi](https://github.com/WaylandAce/mib2q-carplay-rgi)). iOS sends nothing unless the accessory's identification declares route-guidance support. MHI2Q patches in component 0x001E [RE]. The Wireless mod forces `route_guidance = 1` and re-asks on a 3/10/25/55 s watchdog [CODE] (`Wireless: Wireless.java:149-171`, `dipo/RgWatch.java`). `CpTurns` parses the update, maneuver and lane messages into the same `Cluster` calls as AA, including junction type, junction angles, exit angle and linked lanes [CODE] (`Wireless: dipo/CpTurns.java:727-925`). The intersections report shows those angles are parsed but unused, and that Apple Maps fills them while Google Maps mostly does not. The app-side source of these fields is Apple's public `CPManeuver` (junction type and angles, traffic side, road-following names, linked `CPLaneGuidance`, the last from iOS 17.4), so third-party CarPlay nav apps can feed the same messages [DOC] ([CPManeuver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/carplay/cpmaneuver.md); [CPLane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/carplay/cplane.md)). Whether Google Maps and Waze on CarPlay actually populate 0x5202/0x5204 is unverified, and one capture would answer it [RE gap]. CarPlay has no reroute status, so there is no "Recalculating…" [CODE] (`Wireless: docs/ar-hud.md:54`).

### EV routing: the intents path and the Ford path, and why a head unit alone gets only prompts

Apple documents two ways for Maps to get EV data. The **intents path** (iOS 14+) has an OEM iOS app answer `INListCarsIntent` and `INGetCarPowerLevelStatusIntent`. The returned `INCar` lists connectors and max power per connector, and binds to the car through `headUnit`, "the Bluetooth and iAP2 identifiers of the electric vehicle's physical head unit" [DOC] ([INCar](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/intents/incar.md)). Porsche uses it through the My Porsche app [DOC] ([Porsche](https://www.porsche.com/usa/apple-maps-ev-routing/)). The response's `consumptionFormulaArguments` and `chargingFormulaArguments` are undocumented, and a developer building "an aftermarket solution … for any EV" got no answer from Apple [DOC/forum] ([Apple forum 772132](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772132)). The **CarPlay-only path** is Ford's: Mach-E and F-150 Lightning, data "only available on Apple devices connected via Apple CarPlay", developed by Ford and Apple, with preconditioning on the 2026MY Mach-E only [DOC] ([Ford Canada](https://www.ford.ca/support/how-tos/electric-vehicles/other-electric-vehicle-information/how-do-i-use-apple-maps-ev-routing/)).

On the accessory side, iAP2 has identification groups for VehicleInformation (engine type, connectors) and VehicleStatus (range, warnings, charge, max range), plus status messages **0xA100 start, 0xA101 update, 0xA102 stop** [RE] ([JJTech0130](https://gist.github.com/JJTech0130/78527c600f7b4d0a7aeb294eab06d8ba); [DiPlay BYD_NAVIGATION.md](https://github.com/shihabal3amri/DiPlay/blob/v0.2.11/docs/BYD_NAVIGATION.md)). DiPlay sends real BYD data every 30 s. Apple Maps then **offered to find a charging station**, but showed no arrival % and no planned stops. DiPlay does not send the consumption and charging-curve parameters (ids 26/27) [RE] ([DiPlay PR #38](https://github.com/shihabal3amri/DiPlay/pull/38)). A head unit that identified itself as a "Mustang Mach-E" with valid MFi auth got nothing, while a real Mach-E on the same iPhone got the full feature [RE] ([DiPlay #1098](https://github.com/shihabal3amri/DiPlay/issues/1098)). Full Apple Maps EV routing is therefore gated on Apple-side enrollment of the vehicle or OEM app, not on anything a head unit can declare [SPEC, strong]. A companion iOS app that answers the INCar intents with `headUnit` set to our identifiers is a conceivable route. Nobody has shown that Apple lets a non-OEM app do this, and the formula arguments are undocumented [SPEC].

What we can do cheaply already has a home. The Wireless mod publishes a `carplay-identification` API (VehicleIdentity: display name, engine type ELECTRIC, exactly one DC and one AC connector with max power), and its native serializer writes VehicleInformation params 11/12/14 and VehicleStatus params 21/30 [CODE] (`Wireless: src/.../api/CarPlayIdentification.java`, `IdentityInstaller.java:62-101`, `native/identity/identity.c:113-149`). **egmp registers nothing with it**, and no mod feeds live VehicleStatus values [CODE]. LIVI's experience is a useful warning: range-warning updates were ignored until the identification declared `range_warning: true` [RE] ([LIVI #354](https://github.com/f-io/LIVI/issues/354)).

### Cluster video on CarPlay is phone-drawn and not open to us

Since iOS 15.4, CarPlay nav apps can draw into an instrument-cluster scene with `speedLimitSetting` and `compassSetting`, so on CarPlay the speed limit is drawn in phone video too [DOC] ([CPInstrumentClusterController](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/carplay/cpinstrumentclustercontroller.md)). Apple Maps' cluster option arrived in iOS 17.4 for BMW, Volvo and Polestar [DOC/press] ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/02/20/ios-17-4-beta-carplay-instrument-cluster/)). How an accessory advertises a cluster display is not public, and CarPlay Ultra needs Apple-certified OEM integration [DOC] ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/05/carplay-ultra-the-next-generation-of-carplay-begins-rolling-out-today/)). Neither is a realistic aftermarket path.

## Conclusion

The question "what newer protocol features could we add?" has a counter-intuitive answer. The receiver's input is already rich, and the next wins come from reading it fully and describing the car more accurately. Two of our own labels turn out to be guesses that public evidence now contradicts. "Reserve" at 1.2 is probably mass, and "max charge power" at 4.1 is probably max regen power. A third label, "no pack-temp field exists", is simply wrong: 1.8 is battery temperature. Because Maps predicts on the server, these errors never show up as crashes. They show up as quietly worse plans. The real tool we lack is an A/B route harness: a fixed route and SOC, one setting toggled, and Maps' per-stop departure energy and charge time logged from NAVDUMP. Every EV item above depends on it.

The protocol ladder past 1.6 should be treated as a separate research track, not a feature. Its only prize, the energy forecast, mostly duplicates what 0x8006 already gives us. Its price falls on the stock audio and video pipeline that every user depends on. Google's reject list naming Hyundai, Kia and Genesis suggests Google already treats HMG clusters as a special case. Staying on the metadata path, which Google's own docs say feeds "the vehicle's cluster and heads-up displays", keeps us on the route Google supports for all nav apps.

## Car tests for the 2026-11-21 changes

Not run yet. All of these fit in one session: a parked check, a drive of about 1.5 h with a Maps-added DC stop, and a parked check at the end.
- **Parked.**
  - `energy model sent:` lines end with `hvac=… pack=…C`. hvac follows the climate about a minute after it changes, and pack matches the cluster's battery temperature.
  - A route left active for 10 minutes doesn't re-route by itself.
  - Maps' "Compatible plugs" screen lists CCS1 + J1772. CHAdeMO + Other would mean the wire numbers are being read as Maps' enum.
  - With `arrival_charger_pct` = 25, Maps re-plans its stops to arrive with at least 25%. Setting it back to 0 restores the user's own choice. `arrival_dest_pct` works the same way.
- **Drive.**
  - Before leaving, read Maps' planned arrival % at the stop and at the destination with 0.8.2 and with this build, from the same start SOC.
  - Afterwards, compare both with the real arrival %, on a route with some climb.
- **Winter model** (`winter_model` on, cold morning): compare Maps' planned charge minutes and arrival % with the setting off. Watch for re-routes when the heater or the climate changes.
- **NACS** (`ev_connector_nacs` on): Maps' Compatible plugs screen gains NACS, and Superchargers appear as stops.
- **Ioniq 6 (260903):** `sensor 26 sent:` appears, and Maps' battery % matches the dash.
- **Optional, block 4.** The `block4_flat_test` setting overwrites 4.1's powers with a flat 150 kW after 8.1 is computed. If Maps' planned charging minutes move, 4.1 is used as a charge limit. If not, it's regen, as v1.2 says.
